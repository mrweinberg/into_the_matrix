# Into The Matrix — Set Review
**Date:** April 20, 2026
**Comparison baseline:** April 13, 2026 reevaluation (8.3 / 10)

---

## Executive Summary

Three threads of change drive this cycle. First, a wholesale shift from "instant or sorcery" triggers to **"noncreature spell"** triggers across the UR spellslinger axis. Second, a further trim of the **champion** mechanic from 7 cards down to 5 — and a re-reading of UB as the bible's tempo/saboteur archetype rather than a champion-dependent deck. Third, a tight set of patches addressing the structural gaps from the prior review: GW gets both an engine (Docking Bay Alarms) and a relocated lord (Resistance Coordinator), white gains its first true sweeper (Grid Collapse), green's interaction problem is solved with two fight spells (Coordinated Takedown becomes instant; Reality Check replaces Combat Simulation with anti-digital scaling), and the BG archetype description is rewritten to match what the cards actually do.

**Overall Rating: 8.6 / 10** (up from 8.3)

## Category 3: Mechanical Design & Integration — 9.0 / 10 (unchanged, but stronger)

### The Pillars + Sub-mechanics

| Mechanic | Card Count | Density | Change |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Energy ({E}) | 116 | 42.5% | **+5** |
| Digital | 74 references | 27.1% | Stable |
| Jack-in / Eject | 22 cards | 8.1% | Stable |
| Champion | **5 cards** | 1.8% | **−2** (Surveillance Agent, Reinforcement Agent) |
| Noncreature-spell triggers | **6 cards** | 2.2% | **New named axis** |
| Delve | 1 card | 0.4% | Stable |

**What changed:**

- **The "noncreature spell" pivot is the headline move.** Code Wall, Link Monitor, Surge Channeler, Signal Hijacker (common), Mouse, and Apoc all replaced "instant or sorcery" triggers with "noncreature spell" triggers. The trigger surface now includes artifacts (Gun tokens, equipment, vehicles), enchantments (sagas, auras), and lands' own modal effects in addition to instants and sorceries. Concretely: equipping a Gun token now feeds Mouse for {E}{E}, ticks Signal Hijacker, and arms Surge Channeler. This is a much better integration of the spellslinger axis with the rest of the set's artifact-heavy texture.

- **Champion is now a 5-card mechanic.** Down from 8 (April 6) to 7 (April 13) to 5 (now). Surveillance Agent became a +1/+1-counter combat-damage creature with bounce; Reinforcement Agent was replaced wholesale by Incubation Overseer (a {3}{B} 2/2 ETB mill-3 + two Citizens). The five remaining champions are all rares/mythics or signature commons — Agent Brown, Agent Grey, the rare Agent Smith, the mythic Agent Smith Unshackled, and one more. Champion has effectively been demoted from "common-supported sub-mechanic" to "Agent identity flavor on legendary cards."

- **Mouse buffed.** {E} → {E}{E} per noncreature spell. Mouse now ramps energy fast enough to actually fuel its own token-and-loot loop on curve.

- **Energy density up to 116 (42.5%).** Five more cards now generate or spend {E}, mostly via the rebuilt UR spellslinger pieces and Turret Maniac's revised "deal 1 to each opponent + gain {E}" trigger.

**Why 9.0 (held):** The trade is strictly favorable — a parasitic mechanic (champion, requires digital fuel in hand) shrank, a broader trigger axis (noncreature spell) bloomed, and energy density grew without overcrowding. I considered nudging this to 9.5, but the fact that the noncreature-spell axis is still only 6 cards holds it back from a true bump. The pillars remain the same; they just interlock better.

## Category 4: Draft Archetypes & Signposts — 9.0 / 10 (up from 8.0)

Using the archetype names and intents as written in the set bible — not my prior labels — changes the read on UB substantially. The bible defines UB as **Agent Control & Infiltration**: "a control shell that utilizes Digital creatures to play a tempo or control game. It relies on evasive creatures with 'saboteur' abilities (triggers on combat damage)." Champion is upside on Agent rares, not the spine. Under that framing UB is a top-tier archetype, and the champion-thinning of recent cycles is positive. The new GW patches (Docking Bay Alarms + Resistance Coordinator's relocated lord) and BG re-framing close the two largest archetype gaps from prior reviews.

### Deep Dive

**UB Agent Control & Infiltration (8.0 → 8.5):** The depth was always there; I had been reading it wrong. Cataloguing actual saboteur-trigger support:
- **Common:** Surveillance Agent (1/2 digital, +1/+1 counter on damage to player + bounce), an unblockable {2}{U} 1/3 flash Rogue that draws on combat damage, plus the Code Prowler -3/-0 push-through.
- **Uncommon:** Meter Reader (digital menace energy-vampire on damage), an exile-and-cast Rogue, Surveillance Agent's bounce loop, the {2}{U} flash unblockable.
- **Rare:** Mouse (loots on digital damage), Bane (discard + sac on damage), Cain (lifedrain → -X/-X on damage), Agent Jones (scry + duplicates damage triggers — a saboteur multiplier).
- **Mythic:** Thomas Anderson (skulk digital, top-deck filter on damage; Neo's front face).
- **Tempo support:** Code Freeze, Glitch Trap, Viral Overwrite (bounce/freeze/stun); plus Agent Jones doubling every saboteur trigger is itself a build-around.
- **Champion as splash:** Agent Brown, Agent Grey, Agent Smith — open one, you're happy; don't open one, you don't care.

The "Agent Jones doubles every saboteur trigger" interaction is the kind of glue card that holds a tempo archetype together. Combined with digital's natural unblockability against non-digital boards, this is structurally one of the deepest archetypes in the format.

**UR Code Modification (7.5 → 8.5):** The archetype finally has the depth to compete with WU and RW.
- **Mouse** is now a true engine card: {E}{E} on every artifact, instant, sorcery, or enchantment. In a deck with 6–8 artifacts plus removal, that's 12–16 {E} over a game.
- **Apoc's** attack trigger no longer caps at mana value 3 and pays via energy instead of "free" — bigger ceiling, different texture. With Mouse on the board he chains.
- **Pirate-Signal Broadcaster** ({3}{U}{R}, 4/4, end-step {E}{E}, sac+discard for X cards) is a UR rare wheel. Late-game pivot from "drawing nothing relevant" to "redraw your hand with energy fuel."
- **Signal Hijacker** ({2}{R} 1/3 common, +1/+1 counter on noncreature spell) is the common-rarity grower the archetype needed. Repeatable, scales linearly with deck construction.
- **Surge Channeler** trigger now fires off equipment/artifact casts, which UR drafts naturally via Gun tokens and Vehicles.

UR now has the two-tier signpost pattern (Mouse + Apoc + Pirate-Signal at uncommon/rare, Signal Hijacker + Surge Channeler at common/uncommon) that BG got last cycle.

**BR Sentinel Aggro (8.5, held):** Prime Sentinel, The Hunter moved to UR, but the BR signpost spine is intact. **Endless Pursuit** ({1}{B}{R} enchantment) is exactly the rare this archetype wants: every artifact-creature attack pumps for +1/+1, every counter-bearing creature that connects generates {E}, and the {E} feeds the sac-for-burn package. Scrap-Metal Tactician at uncommon plus Endless Pursuit at rare gives BR a clear two-tier signpost structure. The lost rare hurts the bomb count, not the archetype identity.

**GW Zion Resistance (7.0 → 8.0):** The fix for the format's longest-running archetype gap. **Docking Bay Alarms** ({1}{G}{W} enchantment) creates a 1/1 Human Citizen whenever you get one or more {E} during your turn (capped at once per turn). In a 116-energy-card format, this is a self-feeding go-wide engine that doesn't need exotic deckbuilding to fire. **Resistance Coordinator** picks up Reality Advocate's old anthem ("other creatures you control without digital get +1/+1") on top of its existing tap-two-for-card ability, so the lord effect is preserved and the engine slot is freed. GW now has both an engine and a lord, where it had neither. The pair finally has a reason to exist beyond "creatures with vigilance."

**BG Harvester Operations (7.0 → 8.0):** The re-framing matters as much as any card change. The bible description was updated from "death triggers + Energy reanimation" to "the graveyard as a resource, both gaining value as cards fly in and out" — which actually matches what the cards do. The 3-tier signpost stack reads cleanly now: Witness of the Fallen + Hydroponics Tender at common (mill enabler + payoff), Bio-Energy Convertor + Automaton Recycler at uncommon (Ooze engine + Delve sink), and The Source's Defense at rare (top-end mill-and-Robot engine). With Doomed Duo as a white splash for in-color graveyard payoff, the archetype has more depth than I credited.

**GU System Mastery (7.0 → 7.5):** Coordinated Takedown moving from sorcery to instant is the surgical fix the pair needed. Combined with Reality Check (1-mana fight with anti-digital scaling at uncommon), green now has real interaction in mainboard slots, which was the cap on GU's ceiling. Still not a top-tier archetype — the value plan remains slow — but it can now stabilize against fast starts.

**RW Armed and Ready (9.0 → 8.5):** Niobe, Logos Commander was the Mardu equipment lord that read as "RW with a black splash payoff" — drawing a card on every Vehicle/equipped attack with free equip costs for Humans. She's been redesigned into a Naya 2/4 haste / extra-combat finisher with no equipment text whatsoever. The RW archetype loses a top-end card; the new Niobe is generically good for any creature deck but doesn't reinforce the Equipment plan. Turret Maniac's revision (was: 1 damage to a creature; now: 1 to each opponent + {E}) shifts him from spot removal to face damage / energy generator — net neutral for RW Equipment, who already wanted face damage. RW is still the second-best archetype in the set, but it's no longer demonstrably the best.

**RG Bio-Electric Power (7.5, held):** Draconic Sentinel went from a 5/5 flying brick at {4}{R}{R} to a 4/3 menace at {3}{R}{R} that ETBs for {E}{E} and can pump itself to 5/4 trample. Still a power-4+ payoff (when pumped) and still feeds Bio-Current Technician. The redesign is a side-grade for RG: less raw stat, but cheaper, energy-positive, and synergistic. The thin power-4+ payoff density (still only 2 dedicated payoffs) remains the limiting factor.

### Remaining Gaps

- **RG Bio-Electric Power** at 7.5 is now the lowest-rated archetype. Bio-Current Technician + Teachable Pupil + Zion Heavy-Loader is a recognizable spine, but power-4+ payoff density is still thin (only 2 dedicated payoffs). Next candidate for attention.
- **RW lost a top-end signpost.** Niobe's redesign away from equipment matters. Need a new RW rare equipment lord to restore the marquee.
- **Resistance Coordinator may now be overstuffed.** A 1/4 for {1}{G}{W} with a non-digital lord *and* a tap-two-for-card-and-2-life is two uncommons in one card. Worth trimming the lifegain rider.

**Delta:** Three real moves. **Grid Collapse** ({3}{W}{W} sorcery, destroy all creatures + optionally an artifact and/or enchantment) gives white its first true sweeper — an answer to bomb-snowball games (Switch, Agent Smith, Deus Ex Machina) that was missing entirely. **Coordinated Takedown** moving from sorcery to instant turns a fine common into a premium one and solves the worst of green's instant-speed deficit. **Reality Check** ({G} sorcery: fight with 2× damage to digital creatures) gives green a one-mana removal spell with anti-evasion scaling — exactly the answer green needed to the digital combat layer. Turret Maniac's revision (creature-removal → face damage + {E}) is still a small spot-removal loss but is dwarfed by the other additions.

## Category 7: Flavor & Thematic Cohesion — 9.0 / 10 (unchanged)

The noncreature-spell pivot reads as a flavor upgrade as well as a mechanical one: in a set about programs and code, *casting any non-organic spell* (an artifact, an enchantment, a piece of code) feeling like "casting code" is more on-brand than restricting the trigger to instants and sorceries. Pirate-Signal Broadcaster fits the rebel-radio-operator archetype perfectly. Niobe-as-Naya is a defensible flavor read (ship captain coordinating squads) but loses the original Mardu "Equipment commander" feel — a flavor sidestep, not a downgrade.

## Category 8: Bomb & Mythic Design — 8.5 / 10 (unchanged)

No mythics changed. Three rares were redesigned (Pirate-Signal Broadcaster, Niobe, Grid Collapse) and one rare was deleted in flavor (Prime Sentinel, The Hunter — the namespace was reused for Pirate-Signal Broadcaster).

Bomb profile is improved at the margins. **Grid Collapse** is the format's first hard sweeper, and it directly addresses the "Deus Ex Machina is unanswerable" concern from prior reviews — five mana to wipe the board including the 8/8 indestructible isn't a clean answer (indestructible survives), but it does erase the board around it. Pirate-Signal Broadcaster (4/4 dies to common removal) and Niobe (2/4 dies to anything red) remain interactive replacements at similar power level. Holding at 8.5 because Grid Collapse helps with snowballing creature bombs but Deus Ex Machina specifically still walks through it.

## Category 9: Gameplay Variance & Replayability — 8.5 / 10 (up from 8.0)

| Metric | Count | Δ |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Build-around uncommons | 62 | = |
| Modal/flexible cards | ~25 | = |
| Archetype overlap cards (3+ homes) | 33 | = |

**Why up:** The noncreature-spell axis directly addresses the "digital clustering" concern from the April 13 review. Previously, only blue and black drafters meaningfully interacted with the digital sub-format. Now any deck that runs **artifacts** (which is most of them, given Gun tokens and Vehicles) feeds UR-style triggers. This bridges the soft two-format split (digital vs. non-digital) by giving non-digital decks a shared trigger axis with digital decks. RW Equipment in particular now has a partial UR overlap — a Gun token cast triggers Mouse and Signal Hijacker, so a RW splash of red wizards becomes a real draft pivot.

The format now has three intersecting trigger axes (digital matters, energy matters, noncreature spell matters) that drafters can mix and match across pairs. That's better replayability than two axes (digital, energy) where only one was broadly accessible.

Combined with the existing 90+ creatures with some form of evasion, the format now has answers at every speed (instant fight, sorcery wrath, slow token engine).

## Best Parts

1. **GW Zion Resistance finally works.** Docking Bay Alarms (energy → Human Citizen token, once per turn) plus Resistance Coordinator absorbing Reality Advocate's lord effect gives the pair both an engine and a lord in the same archetype slot count it had before. The longest-standing archetype gap is closed.
2. **White gets its first wrath.** Grid Collapse ({3}{W}{W} destroy all creatures + optional artifact/enchantment) directly addresses the "no answer to bomb snowball" problem. Format has a real reset button.
3. **Green's interaction problem is solved.** Coordinated Takedown becoming an instant common fight + Reality Check (1-mana fight with 2× damage to digital) gives green decks both volume and the specific anti-digital tool they lacked.
4. **Noncreature-spell trigger axis.** Six cards now trigger off any non-creature spell. Connects UR spellslinger to artifacts, enchantments, and equipment — bridges the digital / non-digital split that capped replayability.
5. **UB tempo/saboteur depth.** Reading the archetype as the bible defines it (Agent Control & Infiltration, not "champion deck") reveals one of the format's deepest archetypes: digital evasion + saboteur triggers at every rarity + bounce/freeze tempo support + Agent Jones as the trigger-doubler.
6. **BG Harvester Operations re-framed correctly.** The bible description now matches the cards ("graveyard as a resource, both gaining value as cards fly in and out"). The 3-tier signpost stack reads cleanly.
7. **Mouse as a real archetype anchor.** {E}{E} per noncreature spell makes Mouse a draft-defining uncommon and a cross-archetype glue card (UR payoff + UB saboteur enabler).
8. **Champion is finally right-sized.** Five cards, all flavor-critical Agents — splashable upside on top of a tempo deck, no longer a parasitic common-density problem.
9. **Energy density up to 42.5%.** Energy is unambiguously the format's connective tissue, and Docking Bay Alarms now converts it into board presence outside of energy's traditional payoff colors.
10. **Trample up, flying down.** Combined with digital evasion this means the format closes through bodies more than through fliers.

---

## Worst Parts / Areas for Improvement

1. **RW lost a top-end signpost.** Niobe's redesign away from equipment matters. The pair's best rares are now The Virtual Armory and Commander Lock — fine, but Niobe was a marquee. RW needs a new equipment lord rare.
2. **Resistance Coordinator is overstuffed.** A 1/4 for {1}{G}{W} now carries a non-digital lord effect *and* a tap-two-for-card-and-2-life. That's two uncommons in one card. Trim the 2 life from the activation.
3. **Green removal density may have overshot.** Coordinated Takedown (instant common fight) + Reality Check (1-mana uncommon anti-digital fight) potentially makes green the best interaction color in the format, which inverts color-pie expectations. Worth checking total green common removal count after these land.
4. **Green still locked out of digital.** One DFC. Same structural concern. The noncreature-spell axis doesn't help green either, since green is the lowest-instant/sorcery color.
5. **The noncreature-spell axis is only 6 cards deep.** It's a real archetype now, but if one of the six gets cut next cycle the axis collapses. More commons (currently only Code Wall and Signal Hijacker) would harden it.
6. **RG Bio-Electric Power is now the lowest-rated archetype.** Power-4+ payoff density (only 2 dedicated payoffs) is thin. Next candidate for attention.
7. **Surveillance Agent reads cluttered.** A 1/2 for {1}{U} with a damage trigger AND a {1}{U} bounce is two abilities competing for activation. It works inside a tempo deck but the card feels overloaded for a common.
8. **Reality Advocate's name and identity disappear.** Minor flavor cost for a real mechanical gain. Could reuse "Reality Advocate" as the new lord-bearing Resistance Coordinator name to preserve the character.

---

## Conclusion

Three real moves this cycle: the **noncreature-spell pivot** (broadens UR's trigger surface), continued **champion thinning** (now 5 cards, all flavor-critical Agents), and a **structural-gap patch** addressing GW's missing engine, white's missing wrath, and green's missing interaction in a single coordinated set of edits. Reading the archetypes as the bible actually defines them adds a fourth realization: UB was never supposed to be a champion deck — it's a tempo/saboteur shell where champion is splashable upside.

The cost was paid mostly by RW (lost Niobe as an equipment payoff). The new patches add tools without removing established ones — Grid Collapse fills an empty slot (white had no sweeper), Docking Bay Alarms takes Reality Advocate's slot but the lord effect was preserved on Resistance Coordinator, and the green fight spells fill what was effectively dead common space.

Net: +0.3, with three of the four lowest-rated archetypes (GW, BG, GU) all moving up. The format now has 8 archetypes at 8.0+ where it had 5 a week ago. Remaining work is RW's marquee rare, RG's power-4+ depth, the green DFC count, and trimming Resistance Coordinator's stat-line overstuffing.
