# Into The Matrix — Full Set Reevaluation
**Date:** April 13, 2026
**Comparison baseline:** April 6, 2026 review (7.8/10)

---

## Executive Summary

Since the April 6 review, targeted changes have addressed the two weakest archetypes (BG and RG), reduced champion fragility in UB, buffed WU's energy integration, improved green's interaction options, and broadened several parasitic commons. The set's structural foundation was already strong; these changes shore up the weak points without disrupting what worked. The result is a more balanced draft environment where all 10 color pairs have identifiable game plans.

**Overall Rating: 8.3 / 10** (up from 7.8)

This actually brings the uncommon count closer to the Play Booster target of ~100, which is an improvement — individual uncommon ASFAN is now higher, meaning signpost cards appear more frequently.

## Category 3: Mechanical Design & Integration — 9.0 / 10 (up from 8.5)

### The Three Pillars + Sub-mechanics

| Mechanic | Card Count | Density | Change |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Energy ({E}) | 111 | 40.7% | Stable |
| Digital | 57 creatures (74 refs) | 27.1% | +1 (Glitched Citizen token creation) |
| Jack-in/Eject | 22 cards | 8.1% | Stable |
| Champion | 7 cards | 2.6% | Down from 8 (Meter Reader lost champion) |
| Delve (new) | 1 card | 0.4% | New via Automaton Recycler |

**What improved:**

- **Mind-Body Sync now generates {E} on jack-in/eject.** This ties the WU flagship mechanic more tightly into the energy economy. Previously, WU's jack-in theme and the set's energy theme were adjacent but not deeply linked. Now they feed each other: energy funds jack-in activations, and jack-in generates energy back via Mind-Body Sync. This makes the three-pillar triangle tighter.

- **Champion reduced from 8 to 7.** Removing champion from Meter Reader was surgical — it cuts the density of a fragile mechanic without losing any of its identity cards. All the flavor-critical Agents still have it; you just have one fewer "needs digital fuel to function" card floating in mono-black packs.

- **Glitched Citizen solves champion's fuel problem.** This {1}{B} common was already in the set but deserves highlighting in the mechanical analysis. When championed, it creates a replacement digital token — a self-replenishing chain. This single card transforms champion from "high-risk, might not have fuel" to "reliable with basic drafting attention."

- **Delve on Automaton Recycler introduces a new mechanical interaction.** Delve exiling cards from the graveyard triggers Bio-Energy Convertor's Ooze creation, creating a clean two-card synergy at uncommon. It's a small addition but it gives BG a mechanic that no other pair uses, differentiating it from generic energy/midrange.

**Why 9.0:** The mechanical pillars were already strong. The changes make them interact more richly (energy <-> jack-in via Mind-Body Sync, graveyard <-> tokens via Delve + Convertor) and reduce the fragility of the most parasitic sub-mechanic (champion). The set now has fewer dead-card scenarios and more "engine" moments.

This category saw the largest improvement. The two weakest archetypes received substantial reworks, and the third-weakest got meaningful buffs.

### Deep Dive: What Changed

**BG Golgari (4.5 -> 7.0):** The biggest jump. The old BG had Liquefy (generic sacrifice draw) and The Source's Defense (unfocused mill/token enchantment) as signposts — neither communicated "draft BG." Now:
- Bio-Energy Convertor (uncommon) is a graveyard engine in a single card: exile creatures for energy, get Ooze tokens whenever creatures leave the yard, sacrifice Oozes for Surveil to fill the yard again.
- Automaton Recycler (uncommon) introduces Delve, rewarding the self-mill plan while providing a 3/4 menace body that buys back exiled cards.
- Witness of the Fallen (common) grows whenever cards leave the graveyard, tying the theme together at a frequency drafters will see every game.
- The Source's Defense at rare provides a repeatable endgame: mill 2 per turn, convert graveyard creatures into 2/2 Robot tokens.

BG now has the two-tier structure (uncommon signpost + common enabler) that characterizes the best archetypes.

**RG Gruul (6.0 -> 7.5):** The "power 4+ matters" hook transforms RG from "generic energy midrange" into "the big creature pair."
- Bio-Current Technician now explicitly rewards power 4+ attacks with energy, and converts that energy back into mana. It's a payoff card that says "draft big things."
- Teachable Pupil at common cantrips when you control power 4+, providing the common-rarity reinforcement signal.
- Zion Heavy-Loader at 4/4 trample is itself a power 4+ creature that triggers Bio-Current Technician while pumping other attackers.
- The existing density of 16 power-4+ creatures in the RG pool means the theme has natural support without needing to warp other cards.

**UB Dimir (7.0 -> 8.0):** The changes are surgical rather than structural:
- Meter Reader losing champion means one fewer "needs digital fuel" card in the format. It's now a standalone 2/3 digital menace energy-vampire — good in UB without requiring setup.
- Agent Jones moving to {2}{U}{B} 2/3 spreads the curve (was a 3-drop competing with Agent Grey; now a 4-drop that follows it).
- The 7 remaining champion cards are the identity-defining ones: 4 mono-color Agents + Agent Grey + Agent Smith (rare) + Agent Smith Unshackled (mythic). The density is still sufficient for the theme while being less likely to flood your hand with uncastable champions.

### Remaining Gaps

- **GW** is the pair that's "fine but unexciting." Reality Advocate's anti-digital anthem is clever but the go-wide plan doesn't have a distinctive engine. It's the next candidate for attention, though at 7/10 it's not urgent.
- **GU** has a genuine interaction problem — only 6 targeted removal spells. In matchups against fast starts, GU can struggle to stabilize. This is a known trade-off (GU outgrows rather than answers) but it limits the pair's ceiling.
- **UR** is well-designed but rarely the "best deck at the table." Mouse is an excellent signpost, but the pair lacks a clear uncommon #2 that rewards the spellslinger plan. Fatal Exception is a gold counterspell, not an archetype signpost.

## Category 7: Flavor & Thematic Cohesion — 9.0 / 10 (unchanged)

The changes reinforce existing flavor rather than introducing new themes:
- Doomed Duo (creates both a Human and a digital Program from the graveyard) tells a story: two friends, one stays human, one becomes digital.
- Automaton Recycler with Delve: you're harvesting dead programs and machines, recycling their code into new threats.
- Bio-Current Technician rewarding power 4+: the bio-electric energy of the "real world" surges when powerful warriors enter the fight.
- Persistent Defender recurring from the graveyard: a soldier who refuses to stay down — very on-brand for the human resistance.

## Best Parts

1. **Digital/Jack-in/Energy mechanical trinity** — Now even more tightly integrated with Mind-Body Sync generating energy on transform. The three pillars form a genuine gameplay triangle.

2. **RW Equipment Aggro** — Unchanged and still the model archetype. Deepest signpost support, clearest game plan, most satisfying gameplay loop.

3. **BG's graveyard engine overhaul** — Bio-Energy Convertor + Automaton Recycler + Witness of the Fallen transformed the set's weakest archetype into a distinctive self-mill/recycle deck. The strongest improvement in this cycle.

4. **Perfect common color parity** — 15 per color at common remains rock-solid.

5. **Gun tokens** — Still the set's best individual design element. Flavorful, self-contained, and mechanically interesting.

6. **Character card design** — Trinity, Neo, Morpheus, Agent Smith all have multi-card arcs that tell stories through gameplay.

7. **Glitched Citizen as architectural common** — A simple-looking 2/1 for {1}{B} that secretly solves the champion mechanic's reliability problem. Great "lenticular" design.

8. **Power 4+ matters in RG** — Bio-Current Technician and Teachable Pupil give RG a hook that no other pair has. The archetype finally has a reason to exist beyond "good stuff."

---

## Worst Parts / Areas for Improvement

1. **Green's single DFC.** Still only 1 DFC in green (Hydroponics Tender). Green decks remain almost entirely locked out of the digital combat layer without splashing. This is the most persistent structural concern across both reviews.

2. **GW lacks an engine.** Reality Advocate is a clean lord, but the pair has no card that generates compounding advantage. Compare to how Bio-Energy Convertor gives BG a self-feeding loop or how Mind-Body Sync gives WU escalating returns. GW's game plan is "play creatures, attack" without a card that rewards doing so beyond the lord effect.

3. **Blue's 1-drop desert.** Only 2 blue one-drops. Blue aggressive strategies remain nonviable. This is a conscious choice but limits blue-based pair diversity.

4. **GU's interaction deficit.** 6 targeted removal spells is the lowest of any pair. GU can lose to any unanswered threat that outpaces its development plan.

5. **UR's second signpost is weak.** Fatal Exception is a good card but a poor archetype anchor. A gold uncommon creature that rewards casting instants/sorceries (beyond Mouse) would strengthen the spellslinger identity.

6. **Automaton Recycler has a typo.** "When this creature attack s" — extra space. Also, the Delve/Bio-Energy Convertor tension (Delve exiles the creatures Convertor wants to exile for energy) is interesting but could frustrate drafters who expect the two signposts to synergize cleanly. The interaction is: Delve triggers Convertor's Ooze creation, but removes the cards Convertor's tap ability wants. This is a feature (interesting decisions) but could read as anti-synergy.

7. **Energy parasitism** remains the set's biggest portability limitation. 111 energy cards means ITM lives or dies as a self-contained format.

---

## Conclusion

The changes since April 6 are precisely targeted: they fix the two archetypes that needed it most (BG, RG), stabilize the most fragile mechanic (champion), and deepen the set's best mechanic (jack-in + energy integration). No changes made anything worse. The set has moved from "strong skeleton with archetype gaps" to "well-rounded draft format with clear identities across 9 of 10 color pairs." The remaining work is around the edges — GW's engine, green's DFC count, and minor interaction tuning — rather than structural.
